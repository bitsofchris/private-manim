## As recorded

The same vector can have completely different numbers. The numbers are just an address, and that address depends on the directions you pick. Those directions are the basis. A basis is a set of independent vectors that span the whole space. It's the coordinate system that you choose.

Here's the point three, five, two in the usual XYZ. Now swap the basis, but keep the point exactly where it is and let the grid change around it. In this new basis, the same point is minus two, three, two. The arrow didn't move, but its address changed because coordinates are how much of each basis vector you need to get there.

This matters for interpretability work. One, one is a little X and a little Y. Pick a diagonal basis, and it's just one, zero. One clean feature instead of a mix of two.
