## Description

A vector's numbers are an address in a chosen basis. Change the basis: same arrow, new address.

The point (3, 5, 2) in the usual x, y, z: 3 along x, 5 along y, 2 up. Swap the basis and the grid shears around it while the point stays put; its new address is (−2, 3, 2), which is −2 of v1, 3 of v2 and 2 of v3, walked tip to tail to the same spot. Then why it matters for interpretability: (1, 1) is a mix of x and y, but in a diagonal basis it's just (1, 0), one clean feature.
